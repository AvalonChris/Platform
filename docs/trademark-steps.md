# Trademark steps for "Bodyware"

Goal: find out, cheaply, whether "Bodyware" can be used and protected for dietary supplements, before spending
money on labels or domains. Planned for November 2026.

This is a practical guide, not legal advice. Fees and procedures were checked in October 2026; confirm them on
uspto.gov before paying.

---

## What we already know

One live registration for the same word was found:

| | |

| Mark | BODYWARE |
| Serial / registration | 85205308 / 4184967 |
| Owner | Dart Chart Systems, LLC, Cedarburg, Wisconsin |
| Class | 42 (technology services) |
| Covers | Online, non-downloadable software for monitoring and tracking skin diseases and managing records of patients with skin diseases, for the pharmaceutical, life sciences and managed care industries |
| Status | Live, renewed 2022 |

Different goods (B2B medical software vs consumer supplements), but both are in the health space, which is the
risk. Two other similar marks turned up and still need checking: **BODYWARES** (86181863) and **U.S. BODYWARE**
(75124732).

## Step 1: Free search (about an hour)

1. Go to **tmsearch.uspto.gov**.
2. Search each of these: `BODYWARE`, `BODY WARE`, `BODYWARES`, `BODYWEAR`.
3. For every **live** result, note the owner, classes and what it covers in a table like this:

   | Mark | Serial | Live? | Classes | Covers | Concern? |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|

4. The classes that matter most:
   - **Class 5**: dietary supplements, vitamins. Any live BODYWARE-like mark here is a serious problem.
   - **Class 3**: cosmetics and skin care.
   - **Class 35**: retail stores, online retail.
   - **Class 42 / 44**: health software and medical services. Lower risk, like the one above.
5. Also search the web for "bodyware" plus supplements, vitamins and health, to spot unregistered businesses using
   the name.

**Decision**
- A live mark in **Class 5** (or a close one in 3 or 35) means: stop and choose another name. The cursor logo works
  with any word.
- Nothing in 5, 3 or 35 means: go to step 2.

## Step 2: Prepare to file (about an hour)

1. **Decide who owns the mark.** File in the name of the business (for example "Bodyware LLC"), not your personal
   name, if the business is formed by then. A mark filed personally can be assigned to the company later, but that's
   extra paperwork.
2. **Create a USPTO.gov account** and complete its identity verification (an ID check). Allow time for this.
3. **Find the description in the ID Manual** (idm-tmng.uspto.gov). Search "dietary supplements" and use the
   pre-approved wording, Class 5. Writing your own description costs an extra surcharge.
4. **Choose the filing basis:**
   - **Intent to use, Section 1(b)**, if you're not selling under the name yet. You'll later file proof of use
     (a photo of a labelled product or a store page) and pay a further per-class fee.
   - **Use in commerce, Section 1(a)**, if you're already selling. You'll need a specimen at filing: a photo of a
     labelled bottle or a screenshot of the product page with the name and a way to buy.
5. **Have ready:** owner name and address, the mark in standard characters (just the word BODYWARE, so the
   protection covers any logo style), the class and description, and the basis.

## Step 3: File (about 30 minutes, about $350)

1. File through the USPTO's online trademark filing system on uspto.gov.
2. **Class 5 only** keeps it to one fee (base application about $350 per class in 2026). Add Class 35 later if the
   business grows.
3. Mark it as a **standard character mark** (word only, no design).
4. Keep the filing receipt and serial number, and add them to this file.

## Step 4: Wait for the examiner (several months)

- A USPTO examining attorney reviews the application against existing marks, including the skin-software
  BODYWARE, and either approves it or issues an "office action" (a refusal or request).
- **If approved:** it's published for opposition (others can object for 30 days), then registered or, for intent
  to use, allowed until you file proof of use. Use ® only once it's registered; ™ can be used at any time.
- **If refused for likelihood of confusion:** you can respond with arguments (the goods are different), but a refusal
  is also a clear signal to rebrand while it's cheap.

## While the application is pending

- Launching under Bodyware is fine, but keep spending tied to the name small: keep the .co domain, print labels in
  small runs, and don't buy bodyware.com yet.
- To reduce the chance of a complaint from the skin-software owner:
  - don't describe Bodyware as "software" for health,
  - don't sell skin or skin-condition products,
  - keep "ware" as wordplay in the logo and About page rather than a technology claim.
- **Beware of scam letters.** After filing, your details are public and private companies send official-looking
  "fee" or "registration" notices. Real USPTO messages come from the uspto.gov email domain; ignore anything asking
  you to pay a company that isn't the USPTO.

## Free help if you get stuck

- **USPTO Trademark Assistance Center:** free help by phone with forms and process (not legal opinions).
- **USPTO Law School Clinic Certification Program:** participating law schools give free trademark help, with
  students supervised by licensed attorneys. Eligibility and availability vary by school.

## If the name doesn't work out

Changing the brand in the store is one setting (`Store:BrandName` and the email `FromName`); the logo, favicon and
emails follow it. Run any new name through Step 1 first.

**Fallbacks in the same "body as software" style** (web-checked October 2026; trademark search still needed):

| Name | Web check | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cellware | No brand found | "Cell" points at cellular health, the core longevity idea. Several supplement brands start with "Cell" (Cellucor, Cellics, CellCare), so it's a crowded prefix. |
| Wetware | No supplement brand found | Computing slang for the human body and brain. Strong story, but "wet" may put some people off. |
| Somaware | No brand found | Soma is Greek for "body", but "Soma" is also a prescription muscle relaxant and a Japanese pottery style. |

**Ruled out:** Runtime (German nutrition brand entering the US), Uptime (energy drinks and tablets), any "…code" name
including Bodycode (a company owns SOURCECODE, GENCODE, BRAINCODE and other "CODE" supplement marks; "The Body
Code" is also an established healing system), Bodyscript (a fitness body-composition scanner), Daily Build (a
multivitamin product name), Bodystack ("stack" is a generic supplement term, hard to protect).

**Earlier shortlist** (different style): Long Game, Keel, Decade (Decade is close to an existing brand, Decode Age).
